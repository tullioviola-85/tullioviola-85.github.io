# Research

## Philosophy of History, Science, and Culture in the Nineteenth Century

My overarching area of historical specialization is nineteenth- and early twentieth-century philosophy, particularly in North America, France, and Germany. I have studied debates about the meaning of history and the role of culture in American pragmatism (e.g., [Charles S. Peirce](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-philosophical-relevance-of-peirces-historical-studies), [Jane Addams](https://brill.com/view/journals/jph/17/2/article-p328_8.xml?language=en)), the French spiritualist tradition ([Félix Ravaisson, Émile Boutroux](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/57921/chapter-abstract/475497501?redirectedFrom=fulltext)), the German tradition of Kulturphilosophie ([Ernst Cassirer](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-philosophique-2022-4-page-509?lang=fr), [Edgar Wind](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/philosophy-of-culture-naturalistic-or-transcendental-a-dialogue-b)), and more broadly, the exchanges between philosophy and the emerging socio-cultural sciences. My book, [*Peirce on the Uses of History*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110651560/html) (De Gruyter, 2020), analyzes how Peirce understood history as a philosophical tool, linking it to both his philosophy of science and his own historical practice. In other publications, I have examined philosophical reflections on symbols, or the relationship between habit and culture.

## Conceptions of Cultural Transmission: Memory, Heritage, Habit

Closely connected to the themes above, I am also interested in how cultural transmission has been conceptualized between the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, often in ways that bridge cultural and natural sciences. I have written on [cultural memory](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110785845-007/html) and the [philosophy of habit](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110560756-006/pdf?licenseType=restricted). Together with Giovanna Targia (Zurich), I am working on the historical semantics of the concept of "heritage" at the crossroads between biology and culture.

## The Epistemology of Figurative Practices

I'm interested in how our understanding of the social embeddedness of knowledge can be deepened and enriched by attending to channels that are not commonly addressed by epistemologists. One of these channels is images and figurative practices I have studied in particular how nineteenth-century thinkers intertwined philosophical thought with visual practices, and how they theorized this connection. I see integrated history and philosophy of science as a model that could inspire similar work in the humanities.

## Folklore and Epistemologies of Storytelling

My research also turns to another undervalued domain of knowledge-making: storytelling and vernacular narratives. In a [recent article](https://brill.com/view/journals/jph/17/2/article-p328_8.xml?language=en&ebody=full%20html-copy1), I examined Jane Addams's understanding of urban legends in early twentieth-century Chicago. An [NWO-funded project](https://www.nwo.nl/projecten/whbwo82244), begun in 2026, extends this inquiry to other thinkers such as W.E.B. Du Bois, with the aim of highlighting how folk narratives can articulate critical and cognitive perspectives.
